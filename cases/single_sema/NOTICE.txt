Coordinate attribution
======================
Kyle J. Colston, Kyle T. Faivre and Severin T. Schneebeli,
"Permeation enhancer-induced membrane defects assist the oral absorption
of peptide drugs", Nature Communications 16, 9512 (2025).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64891-0
License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

Starting coordinates: Supplementary Data 1, first frame of Supplementary
Video 10. One complete semaglutide was extracted; source atom mappings,
archive checksum and frame checksum are supplied in parameter_source/.

Modifications: removed source CpHMD hydrogens; merged modified Lys/linker
residues; renumbered 31 peptide residues; converted atom names to Amber;
corrected gamma-Glu C23 R geometry to S by reflecting its carboxyl branch;
regenerated hydrogens for the selected fixed protonation state; solvated
and added ions. The FDA identity specifies L-gamma-glutamyl:
https://precision.fda.gov/uniisearch/srs/unii/53axn4nnhx

No complete CHARMM36m/CGenFF force-field topology was present in the public
Supplementary Data 1 archive. The bundled AMBER-derived topology is a
separately generated provisional pilot model, not the authors' force field.

Parameter-generation software/data: AmberTools 24.8 (ff14SB, AMBER atom
types, AM1-BCC, parmchk2, OPC and Li-Merz 12-6 OPC ions), RDKit 2024.09.2,
and ParmEd 4.3.1. The custom charge method, analogies and limitations are
recorded in parameter_source/parameter_audit.json. The single-point
AM1-BCC variant follows the option described in the Amber reference
manual's Antechamber tips (2025, section 16.2, tip 7). It does not establish
solution-state force-field accuracy. Native parameter data retain their
original software/data provenance.
